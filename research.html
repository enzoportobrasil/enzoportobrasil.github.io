Research

One statistical language, several scientific systems.

My work is connected by recurring questions about incomplete observations, rare events, dependence, spatial structure, and how modelling assumptions shape scientific inference. The application areas change; the statistical problem often does not.

Research statement

Start from imperfect observations, look for the structure behind them, and be explicit about what the model still does not capture.

My undergraduate work asked how to reconstruct information when data are missing. The MSc shifted the same concern toward extreme events and spatial dependence: what happens when the scientifically important part of a process lives in the tail, and when neighbouring locations cannot be treated as independent?

That led to Bayesian hierarchical geostatistical modelling, simulation-based assessment, and computational inference in Stan. In parallel, work with solar irradiance and atmospheric variables moved the research toward physical and astronomical observations, where signal, noise, measurement choices, and scientific interpretation are inseparable.
