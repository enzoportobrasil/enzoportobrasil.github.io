Oral presentation18 September 2024

Assessing imputation techniques for missing data using statistical modelsa simulation approach

coBIPE · 1st Brazil–Colombia Binational Colloquium on Probability and Statistics

Universidade Federal do Rio Grande do Norte · Natal, Brazil
Colloquium: 16–18 September 2024

Missing data can hinder statistical analyses that rely on complete datasets. In work coauthored with Eduardo Yoshio Nakano, I explored how regression-based imputation can help, using simulated datasets to compare single and multiple imputation, measures of central tendency, and predictive modelling.

Related research →

We examined the proportion of missing data, the nature of the incomplete variables, and how many variables were affected. We also compared imputation with ignoring or deleting incomplete observations. The study points to the importance of matching the method to the nature and extent of missing data, and brings together the literature and simulation results to offer practical guidance for researchers.

Earlier presentations

Undergraduate research · recorded presentation2021–2022

Imputation techniques for dichotomous variables

Técnicas de Imputação para Variáveis Dicotômicas

PIBIC · Universidade de Brasília

Brasília, Brazil · Slides dated 1 September 2022

Enzo Porto Brasil · Supervisor: Eduardo Yoshio Nakano

My undergraduate research compares mode imputation and logistic-regression imputation for a binary variable in a horseshoe-crab dataset. Repeated simulations introduce different proportions of missing values, allowing us to examine recovery accuracy and the prediction error of a subsequent linear model for shell width.

Related research →

The study found more accurate recovery of the binary values and lower variability in prediction error with logistic-regression imputation. Mode imputation produced a slightly lower mean prediction error in these simulations.
