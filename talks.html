Contributed talk 15 November 2025

Joint extremes of solar irradiance and air densityan EVT and copula approach to tail uncertainty

sys2025 · Systematic and Measurement Errors across the Sciences – AstroStatistics and Data Science

The University of Alabama in Huntsville · Huntsville, Alabama, USA
Workshop: 14–17 November 2025

At sys2025, I presented a study combining NASA SORCE/XPS solar irradiance measurements and their associated uncertainties with air-density estimates derived from INMET observations in Brasília. Using Generalized Extreme Value (GEV) and Bimodal GEV models together with copulas, we investigated the dependence and joint tail behaviour of solar and atmospheric extremes. The work also brought systematic measurement errors into the discussion, connecting uncertainty in space missions records with statistical inference.

Oral presentation 18 September 2024

Assessing imputation techniques for missing data using statistical models a simulation approach

coBIPE · 1st Brazil–Colombia Binational Colloquium on Probability and Statistics

Federal University of Rio Grande do Norte · Natal, Brazil
Colloquium: 16–18 September 2024

Missing data can hinder statistical analyses that rely on complete datasets. In work coauthored with Eduardo Nakano, I explored how regression imputation can help, using simulated datasets to compare single and multiple imputation, measures of central tendency, and predictive modelling.

We examined the proportion of missing data, the nature of the incomplete variables, and how many variables were affected. We also compared imputation with ignoring or deleting incomplete observations. The study points to the importance of matching the method to the nature and extent of missing data, and brings together the literature and simulation results to offer practical guidance for researchers.

Earlier presentation

Undergraduate research · PIBIC · 2021–2022

Imputation techniques for dichotomous variables

PIBIC · University of Brasília

Brasilia, Brazil · Slides dated 1 September 2022

Enzo Porto Brasil · Supervisor: Eduardo Nakano

This undergraduate project compared mode and logistic regression imputation for a dichotomous variable in a horseshoe-crab dataset, using repeated simulations with varying proportions of missing data.

I examined recovery of binary values and prediction error in a subsequent model for shell width. This was one of my first experiences using simulation to understand how missing data decisions shape later statistical analysis and research conclusions.
