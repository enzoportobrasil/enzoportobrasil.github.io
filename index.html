02 / Profile & research

How can we turn uncertainty about the unknown into clear, accessible science?

I came to Statistics at the University of Brasília, drawn to a way of reasoning about uncertainty that could travel across scientific questions. Probability and inference remain my foundation, even as the systems I study change. From early on, I've been driven by the possibility of contributing to what we know about the cosmos. Statistics became my way into that pursuit.

My first research asked what could be recovered when observations were missing. Predictive modelling and simulation led me to a harder version of that question: what can we infer when the observations that matter most are rare, incomplete, or difficult to model?

That question now connects my work on spatial extremes, dependence and environmental data with astrostatistics and cosmology. I don't see these as separate directions, but as different ways of getting closer to the physical systems behind the data.

Statistical Modelling of Spatial Extremes in Complex Systems

How can we model spatial extremes when a single marginal shape is not flexible enough? My MSc dissertation develops a Bayesian hierarchical spatial model with Bimodal Generalized Extreme Value (BGEV) marginals, Gaussian spatial structures and MCMC inference, with an application to extreme events observed in the Amazon.

Inference at the Extremes of the SORCE/XPS Record

What can a finite satellite record tell us when its most unusual observations may reflect both solar variability and the measurement process itself? I study the tails of the SORCE/XPS record to investigate how rare solar behaviour, observational uncertainty, and measurement effects shape statistical inference at the extremes.

06 / Research practice

How I tend to think about a scientific problem

01

What observations can support

I often begin by asking what the observations can support, which assumptions the answer depends on, and what remains uncertain.

02

Simulation as a stress test

Simulation and diagnostics help me examine recovery, calibration and failure modes, especially when the observations offer few ways to check an assumption.

03

The system behind the data

The scientific question and the measurement process help shape my modelling choices. Different problems call for different tools and collaborations.
