Research through-line

“I chose Statistics because I wanted a rigorous language for uncertainty that could travel across scientific questions.”

That path began with missing data and predictive modelling, where simulation made it possible to see how assumptions and incomplete observations alter inference. During my MSc, the same concern moved into spatial extremes, Bayesian computation, dependence, and complex environmental systems.

Alongside the thesis, I started carrying these questions into solar and astronomical data. What connects the projects is not a single application area, but a way of working: build explicit probabilistic models, test them under controlled simulation when possible, quantify uncertainty, and remain careful about the difference between what a model recovers and what observations truly allow us to claim.

Research practice

How I approach scientific problems

01

Inference before decoration

I am interested in what a model says about a scientific question, what assumptions make that statement possible, and how uncertainty propagates into the conclusion.

02

Simulation as a stress test

Controlled simulation gives a setting where the underlying structure is known, making it possible to study parameter recovery, calibration, sensitivity, and failure modes.

03

Move toward the physical system

Rather than treating astronomy, climate, or environmental data as interchangeable datasets, I want the domain problem and measurement process to shape the statistical modelling itself.
