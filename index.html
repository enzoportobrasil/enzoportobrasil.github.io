02Research statement

Statistician trained at the University of Brasília

“I chose Statistics because I wanted a rigorous language for uncertainty that could travel across scientific questions.”

My work begins with a simple discipline: be explicit about what the data can support. That path started with missing data and simulation, then moved into extreme events, spatial dependence, Bayesian computation, and complex environmental systems.

I now carry those questions into solar and astronomical data—not as an astrophysicist, but as a statistically trained researcher interested in how measurement, physical context and uncertainty shape inference.

06 · Research practice

How I approach scientific problems

01

Inference before decoration

Ask what a model says about a scientific question, which assumptions make that statement possible, and how uncertainty propagates.

02

Simulation as a stress test

Use controlled settings to study recovery, calibration, sensitivity and failure modes before trusting observational conclusions.

03

Move toward the physical system

Let the domain problem and measurement process shape the statistical modelling, rather than treating every dataset as interchangeable.
