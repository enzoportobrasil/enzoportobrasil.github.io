MSc in Statistics

Graduate Program in Statistics, University of Brasília (UnB)

Brasília, Brazil
Thesis

“Statistical Modelling of Spatial Extremes in Complex Systems”

Developed a Bayesian hierarchical geostatistical model for spatial extremes with BGEV marginals, allowing all distributional parameters to vary spatially through latent Gaussian structures. The model is designed to capture bimodality, tail behaviour, and spatial heterogeneity within a unified probabilistic framework.

Methods

Bayesian hierarchical modelling; Extreme Value Theory; latent Gaussian processes with spatial covariance; support-preserving parameter transformations; prior specification and regularisation; non-centred parameterisation; HMC/NUTS inference in Stan; spatial confounding control via QR projection; MCMC simulation and diagnostics; parameter recovery; uncertainty quantification; and estimation of extreme quantiles.

Research

Alongside the dissertation, developed independent research in statistical methodology, extreme value theory, climatology, and astrostatistics, resulting in 4 submitted manuscripts. 2 further manuscripts are being developed from the thesis itself, one methodological and one applied. Additional research conducted during the MSc led to 3 oral/invited presentations and 6 scientific posters at national and international events.

Courses

Statistical Inference, Bayesian Inference, Mathematical Statistics, Computational Techniques in Statistics, Machine Learning, Geostatistics, Extreme Value Theory and Copulas, Generalized Linear Models.

Status

Thesis defended. All academic requirements completed. Formal diploma issuance pending.
