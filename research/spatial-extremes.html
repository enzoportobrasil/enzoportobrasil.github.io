Bayesian spatial extremes

Statistical Modelling of Spatial Extremes in Complex Systems

A Bayesian hierarchical geostatistical model for spatial extremes with Bimodal Generalized Extreme Value marginals, latent Gaussian spatial structure, and computational inference through HMC/NUTS.

The question

The Generalized Extreme Value distribution is a standard tool for block extremes, but complex systems can display marginal behaviour that is difficult to represent with a single conventional regime. The MSc thesis asks whether a more flexible extreme-value marginal can be embedded in a coherent spatial Bayesian model while preserving interpretable spatial variation and principled uncertainty quantification.

Model

The proposed model uses BGEV marginals and allows the four distributional parameters to vary spatially through latent predictors with Gaussian spatial structures. Parameter transformations preserve the required support constraints. Bayesian inference is implemented in Stan using Hamiltonian Monte Carlo and the No-U-Turn Sampler.

How it was evaluated

A controlled simulation study was used to assess MCMC behaviour, parameter recovery, uncertainty and recovery of spatial surfaces across multiple scenarios. The real-data application considers quarterly extremes of relative humidity deficit in the Amazon region.

Why it matters to my research

This project brought together several parts of my statistical training—Extreme Value Theory, geostatistics, hierarchical modelling, Bayesian computation and simulation—and made the distinction between model structure and physical system much more concrete. It is the methodological anchor of my current research identity.
