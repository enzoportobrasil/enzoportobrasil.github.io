Statistical Modelling of Spatial Extremes in Complex Systems

Enzo Porto Brasil Universidade de Brasília · 29 July 2026

Read the dissertation ↗ Download PDF ↓

Complete dissertation · Portuguese · PDF · 39.2 MB

Abstract

The Generalized Extreme Value (GEV) distribution is widely used in the modelling of spatial extremes, but it may be limited when representing data from complex systems subject to multiple occurrence regimes. In this work, a Bayesian hierarchical geostatistical model is proposed for spatial extreme values with Bimodal Generalized Extreme Value (BGEV) marginal distribution, allowing the accommodation of bimodality and greater marginal flexibility. In the proposed model, latent predictors associated with the four parameters of the BGEV distribution are specified through Gaussian spatial structures, with appropriate transformations to preserve the parametric constraints of the distribution. Inference is performed using Hamiltonian Monte Carlo, employing the No-U-Turn Sampler algorithm implemented in the Stan software, accessed through R. The performance of the model is evaluated through a simulation study and an application to quarterly extremes of relative humidity deficit in the Amazon region. The results indicate the potential of the methodology for modelling spatial extreme data with bimodal behavior, while preserving a hierarchical structure capable of representing spatial heterogeneity in the marginal parameters.
