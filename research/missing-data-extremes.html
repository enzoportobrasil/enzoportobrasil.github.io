Multiple Imputation Methods under Extreme Values

A methodological project connecting the missing-data questions of my undergraduate work with the extreme-value problems that became central during the MSc.

Where it came from

My BSc thesis used predictive modelling and simulation to compare single and multiple imputation strategies across controlled missing-data scenarios. Later, after moving into Extreme Value Theory, a natural question returned: what happens to conventional imputation procedures when the data contain extreme observations?

Research logic

The project uses simulation to separate the effects of missingness from the effects of tail behaviour. This makes it possible to examine how imputation choices affect downstream inference under conditions that are difficult to diagnose from a single real dataset.

Why it matters to my research

This work makes the continuity in my trajectory explicit. Missing data did not disappear when I started studying extremes; the original question became harder. It also reinforced a methodological habit that appears throughout my work: use synthetic-data experiments to understand what a procedure recovers before trusting it on observations where the truth is unknown.
