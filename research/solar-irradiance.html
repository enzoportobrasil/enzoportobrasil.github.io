Solar spectral irradiance · Extreme value inference

Inference at the Extremes of the SORCE/XPS and INMET Records

How large are the rare increases and reductions in solar spectral irradiance recorded by SORCE/XPS? This research models both tails of daily irradiance changes, with attention to the finite observational record and measurement uncertainty. A connected line of work examines joint extremes with atmospheric variables.

Enzo Porto Brasil and collaborators · University of Brasília (UnB) · 2025–2026

Observing extremes on space missions

The core study uses daily, band-integrated irradiance from photodiode 7 of NASA’s Solar Radiation and Climate Experiment / X-Ray Photometer System (SORCE/XPS), covering 2005–2019. Daily logarithmic changes put relative increases and reductions on a common, dimensionless scale.

The statistical question concerns the tails of this processed record: how to describe unusually large changes, assess the fit, and express their recurrence without overstating what fifteen years of observations can support.

From observations to tail inference

A regular daily series is constructed by linear interpolation. Stationary GEV models are fitted to block extremes by maximum likelihood, with probability weighted moments as a sensitivity check. Autocorrelation and Ljung–Box diagnostics guide the choice of block lengths.

Reported measurement precision and absolute uncertainty inform the interpretation; they are not propagated through the preprint’s likelihood. Gaps, interpolation and the stationarity assumption remain part of the inferential context.

Increases and reductions deserve separate attention

The upper and lower tails are fitted separately, using different block lengths selected from the dependence diagnostics. Return levels describe recurrence under these stationary models, not a schedule of future solar events.

Upper tail · extreme increases

Maxima of daily log-changes are taken over 60-day blocks. The density and quantile diagnostics assess the fit; the return-level curve shows how inference becomes less precise beyond the observed record.

Lower tail · extreme reductions

For 15-day blocks, the minimum daily log-change is multiplied by −1 so that the magnitude of a reduction can be modelled as an upper extreme. Positive levels in this transformed plot therefore refer to decreases in irradiance on the original scale.

The positive fitted shape estimates are compatible with heavy tails under these block constructions. Their difference is descriptive: the unequal block lengths do not support a formal test of tail asymmetry. Long-period return levels depend on extrapolation and do not define physical limits for solar emission.

Connected solar–atmospheric work

Where marginal shape meets dependence

The related presentations pair SORCE irradiance and its reported uncertainty with air density derived from INMET temperature, pressure and humidity observations in Brasília. This is a multivariate extension of the research line, distinct from the univariate SORCE/XPS preprint.

GEV and BGEV fits describe the marginal extremes before copulas are used to model dependence and joint risk measures.
