Extremes of Solar Spectral Irradiance in the SORCE/XPS Record

A project using Extreme Value Theory to study extreme behaviour in solar spectral irradiance measured by NASA’s SORCE mission, bringing statistical methodology into dialogue with physical observations.

The scientific move

Astronomy had been part of my academic interests long before I had a statistical toolkit strong enough to contribute quantitatively. During the MSc, work with solar irradiance became one of the first places where those directions genuinely met: a physical observational record, questions about extreme behaviour, and methods I could interrogate statistically rather than treat as a black box.

Statistical focus

The preprint models extremes of daily logarithmic irradiance changes in the 2005–2019 SORCE/XPS record using GEV distributions. Maximum likelihood fits are checked against probability weighted moments. Long return periods require extrapolation beyond this finite record; reported measurement uncertainty informs interpretation but is not propagated through the likelihood.

Scientific communication

Related solar and atmospheric research produced posters and oral presentations, including an international talk in the astrophysics applications track of Systematic and Measurement Errors across the Sciences: Astrostatistics and Data Science at The University of Alabama in Huntsville.

Why it matters to my research

The project marks a shift from treating a physical dataset as merely another application toward asking how the measurement process, scientific context, uncertainty and statistical model interact. That is the direction I want to deepen in astrostatistics, cosmology and other quantitative physical sciences.
